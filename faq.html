What records should I prepare before speaking to an advocate?

Collect the main agreement, notice, order, claim or property record, plus related correspondence, proof of payment and a dated chronology. Keep originals safe and bring complete copies. The exact checklist depends on the matter; the linked guides provide starting points rather than a complete list for every case.

Does a property title search guarantee clear ownership?

No. A search can review specified records for a stated period, but records may be incomplete and a search cannot rule out every undisclosed claim or factual defect. Ask what documents and dates were checked, what could not be verified and what further enquiries are recommended before committing to a transaction.

Can a motor accident calculator determine compensation?

No. A calculator can provide only an indicative result based on its assumptions and inputs. A claim depends on evidence, applicable law, liability and tribunal assessment. Preserve medical, police, income and insurer records and have the actual claim reviewed before relying on an estimate or accepting a settlement.

Is a notary stamp enough for every document?

No. The receiving institution may require a particular form, witnesses, registration, stamp duty, translation or additional authentication. Ask the recipient what it accepts before signing. Notarisation does not guarantee legal validity, acceptance or the truth of every statement in the document.

Does reading a legal guide create an advocate-client relationship?

No. Website material is general information and is not advice about a person's individual circumstances. Reading a page or using a calculator does not by itself create an advocate-client relationship. A specific matter requires review of its facts and records through an appropriate professional engagement.
