Property Lawyer Pune: Title Search and Due Diligence

Property decisions require careful checking of records, parties and transaction documents. This guide outlines common review steps for a Pune property purchase, sale deed or NRI property matter without treating a document search as a guarantee of title.

General enquiry via WhatsApp

A property lawyer in Pune can help organise a review of the available records, identify inconsistencies for follow-up and explain documentation steps. The precise scope differs for an apartment, plot, inherited property, redevelopment interest or commercial premises. It also depends on the ownership history and the documents that can actually be obtained from the relevant offices and parties.

Buyers should avoid relying only on a seller's photocopies, an online search result or the fact that a document is registered. Registration records an instrument; it does not, by itself, establish that every prior transfer was valid or that no other person has a claim. Obtain independent advice before paying a substantial amount or signing binding terms.

What does a property lawyer in Pune check before a purchase?

A review may examine the available title chain, property description, registration records, revenue or society records where relevant, approvals and known encumbrance information. The scope depends on the property type and records available. A document search is not a guarantee against undisclosed interests, future claims or factual defects.

Is a 30-year title search compulsory in Maharashtra?

There is no one-size-fits-all search period that guarantees clear title for every property. A 30-year review is sometimes requested as a practical scope, but the relevant period depends on the title history, transaction, lender and available records. A lawyer should explain what was searched and what remains unverified.

What is included in property due diligence?

Due diligence can include comparing the proposed agreement with source documents, checking ownership and property identifiers, reviewing available registration and encumbrance information, and identifying approvals or consents that may be needed. It is an evidence-based review, not a title insurance policy or a promise that a dispute cannot arise.

Who prepares and registers a sale deed?

The parties should ensure the deed correctly records the property, consideration, agreed terms and signatories, and then follow applicable stamp and registration formalities. The responsible sub-registrar and required documents depend on the transaction. Legal review before execution is preferable because registration does not cure every defect in title.

Can an NRI complete a Pune property transaction from abroad?

An overseas owner may need to coordinate identity checks, signatures, notarisation or consular authentication, power of attorney and registration requirements. The steps depend on residence country, transaction and recipient authority. Confirm current requirements before signing, and obtain tax or foreign-exchange advice separately where relevant.

Does a title search prove that a property has no dispute?

No search can guarantee that no claim or defect exists. Public records may be incomplete, delayed or silent about an unregistered arrangement or pending issue. A written review should state the records and period examined, assumptions made, gaps found and further checks recommended before the buyer commits.

Property document checklist

Use this as a starting point, not a complete list for every transaction. Ask the seller or relevant authority for legible copies and keep originals available for verification:

  • Prior conveyances and registered instruments relating to the property.
  • Current property description, survey or unit identifiers, and available property-card or revenue extracts.
  • Available society, association or building records and relevant permissions or completion documents.
  • Current tax or dues information and any available encumbrance search records.
  • Identity and authority documents for each owner, representative or signatory.
  • Draft agreement, payment schedule and correspondence recording the agreed terms.

Suggested review process

  1. Identify the property, transaction type and intended completion date.
  2. List the records received and request missing links or certified copies where appropriate.
  3. Compare names, dates, property identifiers, shares and consideration across documents.
  4. Record open questions and obtain official records or third-party confirmations where available.
  5. Review the deed and signing, stamp and registration sequence before execution.

A 30-year title search is not a universal statutory guarantee. Search scope should be set for the specific title chain, property, transaction and client instructions. The written review should disclose limitations rather than imply certainty.
