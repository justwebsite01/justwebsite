Know the difference

Heirship Certificate vs Succession Certificate

People use these names loosely, and banks and offices ask for different ones. In short: a heirship certificate says who the heirs are. A succession certificate lets someone collect money and securities. Probate says a Will is valid and lets the executor act on it.

Why it matters

Why formal succession matters even when the family agrees

Goodwill between family members is real, but banks, registries and buyers cannot rely on it. A formal order turns an understanding into something every institution will accept.

Frozen accounts and demat holdings

Banks, depositories and mutual funds typically hold balances until the legal heirs are established. A succession certificate or probate releases them.

Property still in the deceased’s name

Until mutation, revenue and society records show the dead owner. You cannot easily sell, mortgage, or pay tax in your own name.

Disputes that arrive later

An informal arrangement today can become a claim by a grandchild, a second spouse or a creditor years from now. A formal order fixes who holds what.

Protection from third-party claims

A court-granted certificate or probate protects the bank or buyer who relies on it, which is why they are willing to deal with you.

Legal framework

Which law decides who inherits

In India, inheritance follows the personal law of the person who died, unless they left a valid Will. That is why the first question we ask is about religion and marriage.

Indian Succession Act, 1925

Governs intestate succession for Christians and Parsis, and the law of Wills for most communities. It also contains the rules for probate, letters of administration and succession certificates, which apply across communities.

Wills

  • A Will must be in writing, signed by the testator, and attested by two witnesses (section 63). Registration is optional, but a registered Will is easier to prove.
  • Probate confirms the Will in court. Whether it is compulsory depends on community and place (see the FAQ on probate in Pune).

Christians, without a Will

  • If there are children, the widow or widower takes one-third and the children share the remaining two-thirds. If there are no children but other kindred, the spouse takes half and the rest goes to kindred. If there is no kindred, the spouse takes all (section 33).

Parsis, without a Will

  • The widow or widower and the children take equal shares, and each parent takes half the share of a child (section 51).
Heirs abroad? Domicile matters.Succession to immovable property in India is governed by Indian law. Succession to movable property depends on the deceased’s domicile at death (section 5), so a person settled abroad may be subject to foreign law for movables. We look at both before advising.
Succession certificatesPart X (sections 370 to 390) deals with certificates for debts and securities. The petition under section 372 goes to the District Judge, generally where the deceased ordinarily lived.

Hindu Succession Act, 1956 (amended 2005)

Applies to Hindus, Buddhists, Jains and Sikhs who die without a Will. It does not apply to Scheduled Tribes unless the Central Government notifies otherwise.

Ancestral and self-acquired property

  • Ancestral (coparcenary) property is held by the joint family. Since the 2005 amendment, daughters are coparceners by birth with the same rights and liabilities as sons. In Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020), the Supreme Court held this does not depend on whether the father was alive on 9 September 2005.
  • Self-acquired property belongs to the person who earned or bought it. They can leave it to anyone by Will. Without a Will, it passes under section 8.

If a man dies without a Will (section 8)

  • Class I heirs inherit first and together: son, daughter, widow and mother, plus the children of a son or daughter who died earlier.
  • Class II heirs inherit only if there is no Class I heir. The father comes first; then come brothers, sisters and certain grandchildren, and then more distant relatives in the order set by the Schedule.
  • After that: agnates, then cognates, and finally the government.

If a woman dies without a Will (section 15)

  • Her children and husband inherit first. If there are none, property she inherited from her parents generally goes back to her father’s heirs, and property from her husband or in-laws to her husband’s heirs (section 15(2)).
The heirs families forgetThe deceased’s mother is a Class I heir. So are the children of a child who died earlier. Leaving them out can undo an otherwise agreed settlement, which is why we begin with a careful family tree.
Class I at a glance
Son, daughterEqual shares
WidowShares equally with the children
MotherShares equally with the children
Child who died earlierTheir children take that child’s share together

Muslim Personal Law (Shariat) Application Act, 1937

Directs that Muslim personal law governs intestate succession among Muslims. Sunni and Shia rules differ in important ways, so the school of law matters.

How shares work

  • Inheritance opens on death. There is no idea of a share “by birth” in ancestral property.
  • Sharers receive fixed fractions. Residuaries take what remains. Distant kindred inherit only if neither exists.
  • As a general rule, a son’s share is twice a daughter’s.

Wills

  • A Muslim can leave at most one-third of the net estate (after funeral costs and debts) by Will without the heirs’ consent. The rest passes by the fixed shares.
  • Under Sunni law, a bequest to an heir generally needs the other heirs’ consent.

Agricultural land can be subject to state-specific rules. We check this early.

Special Marriage Act, 1954 and customary laws

Some families fall outside the standard religion-based rules. These are the common cases.

Marriage under the Special Marriage Act

  • For a marriage solemnised under this Act (including inter-faith marriages), succession to the property of the spouses and their children is generally governed by the Indian Succession Act, 1925 (section 21).
  • If both spouses are Hindu, Buddhist, Sikh or Jain, the Hindu Succession Act continues to apply.

Customary and local laws

  • Scheduled Tribes are outside the Hindu Succession Act unless notified, and customary tribal law may govern.
  • Goa follows its own civil code, which treats marital property differently from the rest of India.
  • Mixed or converted families raise questions of which personal law applies. The answer depends on the facts and sometimes on court decisions.

Documents

The master document checklist

Don’t have everything yet? Start anyway. We help trace and replace missing papers. Open a group below, or build a checklist for your own situation.

The deceased
  • Death certificate: the original, plus about five certified copies
  • Aadhaar, PAN and one more ID (passport, voter ID or driving licence)
  • Proof of last place of residence (utility bill, ration card or Aadhaar address)
  • Any Will, codicil or nomination papers you can find, even if unsure they are valid
The applicant and the family
  • Your own ID and address proof, and passport-size photographs
  • Proof of relationship: birth certificate, school leaving certificate, marriage certificate or ration card
  • A family tree naming every legal heir: full name, age, relationship, address, and whether alive
  • Death certificates of any heir who died before the deceased
  • Consent or no-objection affidavits from other heirs who agree (we draft these)
The assets
  • Bank: passbooks or statements, account numbers, FD receipts, locker details, nomination and joint-holder details
  • Shares and mutual funds: demat statement or client master list, share certificates, folio numbers, latest consolidated account statement
  • Insurance, provident fund and pension: policy documents, premium receipts, nomination, UAN, pension payment order
  • Immovable property: registered title deeds, 7/12 or 8A extract or property card, latest tax receipt, society share certificate
  • Vehicle: registration certificate and insurance policy
  • Business interests: partnership deed, registrations, share certificates of private companies
  • Liabilities: loan and credit card statements, since debts are settled from the estate
If there is a Will
  • The original Will and any codicils. Please keep it unfolded and unmarked, and bring it to us as it is
  • Names and addresses of the executor and attesting witnesses, and whether they are alive
  • Registration receipt, if the Will was registered
If an heir lives abroad
  • Passport copy, attested by a notary or the Indian embassy or consulate
  • Overseas address proof
  • A power of attorney executed abroad before a notary and apostilled (or attested by the Indian mission), authorising someone in India to act
  • NRE or NRO account details for receiving funds. Repatriation of inherited money is subject to FEMA rules, and we advise on this

Questions families ask

Straight answers to common questions

How do I get a succession certificate for a bank balance?

A succession certificate is granted by the District Court under Part X of the Indian Succession Act, 1925. It lets the named heir collect the deceased’s bank deposits, fixed deposits, shares and similar debts and securities.

The usual sequence is: get the death certificate; ask the bank in writing what it requires; prepare a petition under section 372 listing every legal heir and every account; file it in the District Court where the deceased ordinarily lived; go through notice, any objections and a short hearing; and then receive the certificate, often after giving an indemnity bond or security. Uncontested petitions commonly take several months.

What is the difference between probate and a legal heir certificate?

A legal heir certificate (also called a heirship certificate) is an administrative document from the revenue office that records who the surviving legal heirs are. Probate is a court order confirming that a Will is genuine and authorising the executor to act on it.

The first answers “who are the heirs?”. The second answers “is this Will valid, and who may carry it out?”. If there is no Will, probate does not arise. The court grants that apply are then a succession certificate (for money and securities) or letters of administration.

Is probate compulsory in Pune?

For Hindu, Sikh, Jain and Buddhist Wills made, and relating to property, outside Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai, probate is generally not compulsory by law. In practice, banks, registrars, buyers and co-operative societies often insist on it, and probate gives the Will the court’s endorsement.

For Christian and Parsi Wills, probate is generally needed to establish rights as executor or legatee in court. Muslim Wills follow Muslim law and are not subject to the same requirement. We confirm the position for your Will before recommending a route.

Do we still need a succession certificate if the account has a nominee?

Often yes, or at least it is the safer route. A nominee is the person the bank or company can safely pay, but nomination alone does not generally decide who owns the money. The legal heirs, or the people named in a Will, can still have a claim to it. Life insurance has special rules that can give close-family nominees stronger rights.

Many banks release funds to a nominee against an indemnity. If the balance is large, or the heirs disagree, a succession certificate or probate is the better protection.

What happens to a joint bank account when one holder dies?

The surviving holder can usually continue to operate the account and be paid, depending on the mandate (for example “either or survivor”). That is a rule about who the bank can safely pay. It does not by itself settle who is finally entitled to the money.

If the funds were the deceased’s, the heirs can still have a claim, and the survivor may be asked to account for it. Take advice before spending or moving large sums.

Who inherits if there is no Will?

The law of the deceased’s religion decides. For Hindus, Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists, the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 applies. A man’s property goes first to his Class I heirs together: widow, sons, daughters and mother, along with the children of a child who died earlier. If there are none, Class II heirs follow, beginning with the father. For Muslims, fixed Shariat shares apply. For Christians and Parsis, the Indian Succession Act, 1925 applies.

Because heirs can include people families overlook, such as the deceased’s mother or the children of a predeceased child, a careful family tree is the first thing we draw up.

The original Will cannot be found. What can we do?

Start with a thorough search: the deceased’s papers, bank lockers, their advocate or chartered accountant, and the Sub-Registrar’s office if the Will may have been registered. Keep any copy or draft you find.

If the original is lost, a court can sometimes allow its contents to be proved by other evidence, but the bar is higher and the petition needs careful drafting. A Will that cannot be proved is treated as if it did not exist, and the estate passes as if there were no Will.

I am an NRI. Can I deal with this from abroad?

Largely, yes. Many steps can be done by an authorised representative in India under a power of attorney that you sign before a notary abroad and have apostilled (or attested by the Indian embassy or consulate). Affidavits can be prepared the same way.

If the court needs your evidence, it may allow it by affidavit or by video-conferencing, at the judge’s direction. Moving inherited funds out of India is subject to FEMA rules, and we advise on that alongside the succession work.

Do daughters have equal rights in ancestral property?

Yes. Since the 2005 amendment to section 6 of the Hindu Succession Act, a daughter is a coparcener in joint family property by birth, with the same rights and liabilities as a son. In Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020), the Supreme Court held that this does not depend on whether the father was alive on 9 September 2005.

Daughters are also Class I heirs to a father’s self-acquired property if he dies without a Will. A father can, however, leave his self-acquired property to anyone by Will.

How long does it take?

It depends on the route and on whether anyone objects. As a general guide, a legal heir certificate from the revenue office often takes 15 to 45 days. An uncontested succession certificate or probate typically takes about 4 to 9 months in court. Contested matters take longer, sometimes years.

These are estimates, not promises. Court calendars and office practice vary.

How much does a succession certificate cost in India?

The cost has several parts: an ad valorem court fee on the value of the debts and securities claimed (set by each state’s court-fee law); the indemnity bond or security the court may require; notice and publication charges; and the advocate’s fee. Because the court fee depends on the value of the assets, there is no single figure.

After reviewing your asset list, we give you a written estimate of the likely costs and timeline before you decide to proceed.

Can one heir apply on behalf of the others?

Yes, one or more heirs can apply, but the petition must disclose all the legal heirs, and the court notifies them. A certificate granted to one heir lets that person collect the assets. It does not take away the other heirs’ shares, and the money must still be shared correctly.

If heirs disagree about their shares, a separate partition or declaration may be needed.
