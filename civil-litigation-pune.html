Civil Lawyer in Pune: Partition, Injunction, Specific Performance and Property Suits

Civil disputes affect property, contracts, money and family interests, and strict time limits apply. This page explains how civil cases work in Pune courts, which remedies may be considered, how limitation works and what to prepare before consulting an advocate.

General enquiry via WhatsApp

Dhende Associates, established in 1984 at Pune, advises on and conducts civil proceedings, including title and property disputes, partition, injunctions, enforcement of agreements, recovery and succession-related claims. Whether a suit is appropriate depends on jurisdiction, limitation, evidence, the parties and the exact relief sought. Not every dispute belongs in court, and a good first review will tell you that honestly.

Key points at a glance

  • Check limitation first. Most civil claims are barred if filed late; the clock often starts before people realise.
  • Documents decide cases. Title papers, stamped and registered agreements, payment proof and notices carry more weight than recollection.
  • Interim relief is separate from the final decree. A temporary injunction needs a prima facie case, balance of convenience and irreparable harm.
  • Never ignore a summons. A court can proceed ex parte if no one appears.
  • Settlement is always an option. Mediation, Lok Adalat or negotiation can end a dispute sooner and at lower cost.

Before filing: is court the right route?

  1. Send a legal notice where appropriate. It records your demand and the other side's response. It is mandatory in some cases, for example a notice under Section 80 CPC before suing the Government or a public officer for acts in official capacity.
  2. Consider mediation or settlement. Pre-institution mediation is compulsory for commercial disputes under Section 12A of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015, unless urgent interim relief is sought. For other civil disputes, the Mediation Act, 2023 provides a statutory framework, and courts may refer parties to ADR under Section 89 CPC. Lok Adalats and the Pune legal services authorities also facilitate settlements.
  3. Identify the right forum. Jurisdiction turns on the subject matter, the value of the claim, where the property lies or the defendant resides, and special statutes. Agricultural land and tenancy matters may involve revenue authorities or special tribunals, so title disputes are not always for a civil court alone.
  4. Confirm limitation. A time-barred suit is dismissed regardless of merit.

Stages of a civil suit

  1. Plaint. Facts, cause of action, jurisdiction, valuation and relief are set out, with court fee paid under the Maharashtra Court Fees Act, 1959. A plaint can be rejected at the threshold under Order VII Rule 11 CPC if it discloses no cause of action or is barred by law.
  2. Summons and service. The defendant is summoned and must appear. Service can take months and is a frequent source of delay.
  3. Written statement. Generally due within 30 days of service, extendable by the court to a maximum of 90 days (Order VIII Rule 1 CPC). Commercial suits have a 120-day outer limit.
  4. Interim applications. Injunctions, attachment before judgment, appointment of a receiver or commissioner, and similar relief, decided on affidavits and documents.
  5. Framing of issues. The court identifies the questions that must be decided.
  6. Evidence. Examination-in-chief by affidavit, cross-examination and exhibiting of documents. For proceedings begun on or after 1 July 2024, the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 applies; earlier proceedings continue under the Indian Evidence Act, 1872. Electronic records need the prescribed certificate for admissibility.
  7. Final arguments and judgment. A decree follows the judgment and states the relief granted.

Injunctions and interim relief

A temporary injunction under Order XXXIX Rules 1 and 2 CPC preserves the position until the suit is decided. Courts generally examine three things: a prima facie case, balance of convenience and irreparable injury that damages cannot repair. Delay, concealment of facts and unclean conduct weigh against the applicant. A perpetual injunction is granted at the end of the trial under Sections 37 and 38 of the Specific Relief Act, 1963, and Section 41 lists cases where an injunction cannot be granted.

Orders must be obeyed. Disobedience can invite proceedings under Order XXXIX Rule 2A CPC, including attachment of property and civil imprisonment. If you receive a notice of an injunction application, take advice before the hearing date.

Partition suits

A partition suit asks the court to declare each co-owner’s share and separate it. In Hindu undivided families, daughters have the same coparcenary rights as sons by birth under Section 6 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956 as amended in 2005, as explained by the Supreme Court in Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma (2020). Other communities follow their own personal law or the Indian Succession Act, 1925.

  • Who must be joined: all co-owners and heirs, and often purchasers from a co-owner.
  • What the court examines: source of title, whether the property is ancestral or self-acquired, wills, earlier partitions, transfers and possession.
  • Two stages: a preliminary decree fixes shares; a final decree divides the property by metes and bounds, often with a court commissioner.
  • Agricultural land: revenue laws such as the Maharashtra Land Revenue Code and fragmentation restrictions may apply alongside the suit.

Specific performance of agreements

After the Specific Relief (Amendment) Act, 2018, specific performance is no longer treated as a purely discretionary remedy. The court must grant it where the statutory conditions are met, subject to the exceptions in Section 14. The plaintiff must prove that they have performed, or have always been ready and willing to perform, their essential obligations (Section 16(c)), and must file within limitation. Typical issues in agreements to sell include proof of payment, time being of the essence, the seller’s title, encumbrances, stamping and registration, and whether the buyer sought the relief promptly after breach. In a suitable case, the alternative of refund with damages is available.

Documents and evidence

Prepare a one-page dated chronology first: the event that created the dispute, each written communication, payments, notices, earlier proceedings and any hearing date. Then organise:

  • Agreements, title deeds, 7/12 extracts, property cards, mutation entries and receipts. Check that instruments are duly stamped under the Maharashtra Stamp Act, 1958 and registered where the law requires, as defects can affect admissibility and enforceability.
  • Legal notices, replies, emails, messages and postal or delivery acknowledgements.
  • Summons, pleadings, orders and case numbers (CNR) for any current or earlier proceeding.
  • Photographs, survey plans, measurement reports or expert opinions on the disputed issue.
  • Names and addresses of all parties and witnesses.
  • Any settlement discussions and deadlines set by a court or authority.

Keep originals safe, do not annotate or discard them, and preserve electronic records in their original form.

Appeals and execution

A decree can be challenged by a first appeal under Section 96 CPC, which examines facts and law, and by a second appeal to the High Court under Section 100 CPC only on a substantial question of law. Appeal periods are short, so act immediately after judgment. A decree has value only when executed: execution proceedings under Order XXI CPC can cover delivery of possession, attachment and sale of property, and recovery of money.

Costs and timelines

Cost has three parts: court fee (often based on the value or the relief), professional fees and incidental expenses such as commissioner and expert charges. Timelines vary with court workload, service of summons, interim applications, evidence and adjournments. A lawyer can outline the likely stages and a realistic range after reviewing your papers, but no advocate can ethically promise a date or an outcome. You can follow case status through the eCourts services using the CNR number.

A lawsuit is not the only way to resolve a civil dispute. Notice, negotiation, mediation, statutory remedies or a response to an existing proceeding may be more suitable. Court outcomes and timelines cannot be guaranteed.

Frequently asked questions

What does a civil lawyer in Pune handle?

A civil lawyer handles disputes over private rights: property and title, partition, injunctions, contracts and specific performance, recovery of money, inheritance and tenancy-related claims. The correct court, remedy and evidence depend on the parties, subject matter, value, location and applicable law, so a preliminary document review should come first.

How is a civil case different from a criminal case?

A civil case enforces or protects private rights and usually ends in a decree such as a declaration, injunction, possession, partition or money. A criminal case concerns offences against the State and can end in conviction or acquittal. Some disputes, such as a dishonoured cheque or a cheating allegation, can involve both tracks.

When might a partition suit be considered?

A partition suit may be considered when co-owners or heirs claim competing shares in joint or inherited property and a settlement is not workable. Title, personal law, earlier transfers, possession and the identity of every interested party must be checked first. Shares cannot be fixed from family relationship alone.

When can a court injunction be requested?

A temporary injunction under Order XXXIX of the Code of Civil Procedure is generally considered where the applicant shows a prima facie case, balance of convenience and likelihood of irreparable harm. Urgency alone is not enough, and the court looks at delay and conduct. Do not take self-help steps or ignore a court notice without advice.

Can a court order specific performance of an agreement?

Specific performance of a contract is available under the Specific Relief Act, 1963, as amended in 2018, subject to the statutory exceptions. The plaintiff must generally prove continuous readiness and willingness to perform, and the suit must be filed within limitation. A signed agreement alone does not guarantee the remedy.

What is the limitation period for a civil suit?

Limitation depends on the type of suit under the Limitation Act, 1963. As a general guide, suits for specific performance, declaration and cancellation of an instrument are commonly three years from the relevant date, while suits for possession of immovable property based on title are twelve years. The starting date is fact-specific and must be verified.

What documents should I bring to a civil lawyer?

Bring the agreement or title papers, payment proof, notices and replies, court papers with case numbers, relevant emails and messages, and a dated chronology with names and addresses of the other parties. Keep originals safe and do not alter or write on records that may be needed as evidence.

Is mediation compulsory before filing a civil suit?

Pre-institution mediation is mandatory for commercial disputes under Section 12A of the Commercial Courts Act, 2015, unless urgent interim relief is sought. For ordinary civil disputes it is generally voluntary under the Mediation Act, 2023, although courts may refer parties to mediation or other ADR under Section 89 of the Code of Civil Procedure.

How long does a civil case take in Pune?

There is no fixed duration. Time depends on court workload, service of summons, interim applications, evidence, adjournments, settlement efforts and appeals. A lawyer can explain the likely stages after reviewing the case but cannot promise a date or result.
